▣ What it is & how it works

A stock represents a partial ownership interest in a company. When you buy shares, you become a shareholder and the value of your investment can rise or fall as the market price changes. Some companies also distribute part of their profits to shareholders through dividends. Publicly traded stocks can generally be bought and sold through financial markets.

▦ Example

You buy 100 shares at ₹500 each. Your initial investment is ₹50,000. If the share price rises to ₹700, the market value becomes ₹70,000, producing a ₹20,000 (40%) capital gain, before applicable costs and taxes. Any dividends received would be additional return.

↗ Potential Rewards

  • Capital appreciation
  • Dividend income
  • Ownership in businesses
  • Potential long-term wealth creation

⚠ Key Risks

  • Share price can fall
  • Company can perform poorly or fail
  • Market volatility
  • No guaranteed returns

♧ Best suited for

Investors who can tolerate market fluctuations and have a medium- to long-term investment objective. It may be less suitable for someone seeking guaranteed returns or very predictable short-term outcomes.

How returns are generated

Returns can come from increases in the market value of shares and, for companies that distribute them, dividend payments.

Costs

Depending on the market and method of investing, costs may include brokerage, transaction charges, taxes, exchange fees and other applicable charges.

Tax considerations

Tax treatment depends on the country, investment type, holding period, transaction and applicable tax laws. Tax rules can change and should be verified against current official sources.

Availability

Publicly traded stocks are available in many countries through regulated financial markets. Available securities, market access and trading rules vary by jurisdiction.

Important considerations

  • Past performance does not guarantee future returns.
  • Individual stocks can lose a substantial portion or all of their value.
  • Risk differs significantly between companies.
  • Liquidity varies between individual stocks and markets.
